## Claim

Contrastive pairs drawn from `contradicts` and `supports` links produce better domain classification fine-tunes than randomly sampled pairs from the same corpus.

## Operationalization

Dataset: legal corpus of 10K contracts with AI-classified links.
Metric: F1 on clause-type classification and contradiction detection.
Baseline: fine-tune on randomly sampled block pairs.
Intervention: fine-tune on pairs drawn exclusively from `contradicts` and `supports` links (matched by doc).
Acceptance criterion: F1 improvement > 5% on held-out contradiction detection task.

## Null Hypothesis

Graph-derived contrastive pairs provide no significant F1 improvement over random sampling; the link structure encodes no useful training signal beyond corpus co-occurrence.

## Experiment Spec

1. Take the legal corpus. Extract all AI links with `rel_type IN ('contradicts', 'supports')` and confidence > 0.7.
2. Build two training sets of equal size: (a) random block pairs, (b) link-derived contrastive pairs.
3. Fine-tune a base encoder (e.g., sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2) on both with InfoNCE loss.
4. Evaluate both on a 500-sample held-out set with human-labeled contradiction pairs.
5. Compare F1.
