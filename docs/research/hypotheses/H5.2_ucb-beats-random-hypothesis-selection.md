## Claim

A UCB-based hypothesis selection policy outperforms random hypothesis selection in terms of information gain per experiment, measured over 20 cycles on a benchmark where ground truth is known.

## Operationalization

Dataset: the Nexum hypothesis store itself (self-referential); or a synthetic bandit benchmark where each hypothesis has a known ground-truth answer probability.
Metric: cumulative information gain (bits) after N cycles; alternatively, number of hypotheses correctly resolved (supported or contradicted) after N cycles.
Baseline: random selection policy (uniform over untested/inconclusive hypotheses).
Intervention: UCB policy with c=1.4.
Acceptance criterion: UCB resolves > 20% more hypotheses correctly than random over 20 cycles.

## Null Hypothesis

Hypothesis difficulty is too uniform for UCB to exploit; random selection performs equivalently.

## Experiment Spec

1. Create a synthetic benchmark: 20 hypotheses each with a known "true" answer probability (sampled from Beta(2,2)).
2. Simulate 20 cycles of random selection. Each cycle: select hypothesis, simulate experiment outcome by sampling from its true probability. Record cumulative correct resolutions.
3. Repeat with UCB (c=1.4). Track visit counts and posterior estimates.
4. Run 100 simulations of each policy. Compare mean cumulative correct resolutions at cycle 5, 10, 15, 20.
